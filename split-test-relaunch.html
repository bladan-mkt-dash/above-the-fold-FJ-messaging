Link CTR and CPC

CTR is link clicks ÷ impressions. CPC is spend ÷ link clicks. Image 5 is the click-rate winner. Image 4 is the cheapest click. Image 6 sits between them.

Y-axis: link CTR (%).

Y-axis: CPC (USD per link click).

Image 5 wins CTR

1.113% link CTR vs 0.881% (Image 6) and 0.742% (Image 4). The CTR gaps are significant (p < 0.001 vs Image 4, p = 0.005 vs Image 6).

Image 4 wins CPC

$0.35 per link click vs $0.43 (Image 6) and $0.51 (Image 5). Same budget, more clicks, cheaper clicks.

Oct 10 pulled Image 4’s rate down

That day Image 4 bought 34,868 impressions at 0.37% link CTR and $0.18 CPC. On Oct 7 and Oct 8 its link CTR was the highest of the three, at 2.47% and 2.55%.

Daily link CTR

Oct 6 is a partial afternoon. Oct 7–9 read as a headline test. On Oct 10 Image 4 found cheap inventory and its CTR collapsed.

Y-axis: link CTR (%). Oct 6 starts at 12:13 PM ET.

Daily CPC

Image 4’s CPC fell with that cheap inventory on Oct 10. Image 5’s CPC rose across the five days.

Y-axis: USD per link click. Oct 6 starts at 12:13 PM ET.

Daily impressions

Spend stays even because each ad set has its own $30 daily budget. Impressions do not. Meta bills for impressions and bids lowest cost for a link click, so the same dollars buy as many impressions as the auction will sell. On Oct 10, Image 4 spent $22.96 and received 34,868 impressions at a $0.66 CPM. Image 5 spent $25.88 and received 3,650 impressions at a $7.09 CPM. Image 4 did not receive extra budget. Its inventory was cheaper, which is also why its link CTR fell to 0.37% and its CPC fell to $0.18 that day.

Y-axis: impressions. Oct 6 starts at 12:13 PM ET.
